What it does

Since January 2026, New Zealanders can build a granny flat up to 70m² without a building consent. That means no council inspector checks the work — but it still has to meet the Building Code. The Granny Flat Checklist puts you in control: plain-language, stage-by-stage self-checks you complete on site with your LBP, from siting to final, each with a signed PDF record. Written by a former council building inspector, approved under Regulation 18 of the Building (Accreditation of Building Consent Authorities) Regulations 2006 for Residential 3 and Commercial 1 work.

The risk with exempt work

Exempt building work still has to meet the Building Code — nobody signs off before you build, which means nobody catches a mistake until it's already in the ground or under the cladding. This checklist walks you through what a building inspector actually checks, stage by stage, before you close each one up.

Who wrote these checklists

The author started as a carpentry apprentice, ran his own building business for seventeen years, then spent eleven years as a building inspector, approved under Regulation 18 for Residential 3 and Commercial 1 work. He now teaches building surveying and compliance at the training organisation that developed, with BOINZ, the NZQA-approved New Zealand Diploma in Building Surveying (Level 6) — the qualification built specifically for building consent authority officers (processors, inspectors and consent certifiers) to meet Regulation 18. Every check in this system is something he has looked for on a real site.
